Sun path

A WebGL study · Puriya Mirza

The goal

A shimmering ocean made of dots

I wanted an ambient, animated background for my site: tiny flickering dots that move like waves. It had to feel organic and cost almost no processing power.

The result is the page you're on. A full-page WebGL water study that runs at 24 fps with almost no CPU cost, shows a still frame for people who prefer reduced motion, and pauses when the tab is hidden.

~18,600dots per screen 24frames per second ~8 ms → <0.1 msCPU per frame, canvas to WebGL

Process

Six steps from sketch to sea

  1. Three ways to build it. I compared a CSS gradient sweep, a CSS grid of fading dots, and a canvas drawn with sine-wave math. The CSS options were nearly free to run but too rigid. Only the canvas could truly flicker.
  2. Sea states. I tuned the canvas into calm, swell and choppy. Choppy came closest, but it still read as a diagram of waves.
  3. A real reference. I filmed open water and studied the frames. An angled view, dark near water, silvery far water, and glints where ripples tilt toward the light made it read as water, so I rebuilt the model around those.
  4. Variations. I generated six versions with swells, gust patches, wind streaks and a sun-glitter column, tuned glint density by distance from the horizon, and picked the sun path.
  5. Moving it to the GPU. I ported the winner to WebGL so every dot is calculated in parallel.
  6. Refining it on the real site. I zoomed out and ran the water down the page. The first screen looks to the horizon, the view tilts straight down as you scroll, and the page ends at the side of a ship with foam breaking along the hull.

What I learned

Shimmer comes from choppiness

  • Real footage beats tweaking. One short clip did more than hours of adjusting numbers.
  • More randomness wasn't more organic. A noise-warped, 12-wave version looked worse and lost its shimmer, so I reverted it.
  • Shimmer comes from choppiness, not smoothness. At 60 fps glints glide. At 24 fps they pop on and off, and that pop is what reads as sparkle.
  • Prototype cheap, port once. I explored in canvas, where changes were quick, and moved only the final design to the GPU.
